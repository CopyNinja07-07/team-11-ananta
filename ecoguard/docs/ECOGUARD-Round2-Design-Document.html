ECOGUARD in plain words

If you read one page, read this one.

What it is

  • ECOGUARD is software plus small sensor boxes that find the electricity and water a building wastes.
  • It names the room, the cause, the cost in rupees and the fix.
  • It was built for Academic Block A in Jammu: three floors, eight rooms, washrooms, a sump, a pump and a roof tank.

What it watches

  • Power in every room, compared with what the room should use for the people inside.
  • The pump, the tanks and the water flow on each floor.
  • Two kinds of waste that no meter shows: heat from loose wiring inside the walls, and damp walls leaking current.

What it does

  • Raises an alert with a plain explanation and one-tap fix.
  • Can act on its own: switch an empty room off, reset an AC, stop the pump, close a valve.
  • Never switches off a room with people in it.

How we know it works

  • A full simulated year with 558 hidden faults: 95% caught, 99% of alerts correct.
  • The same year run with and without ECOGUARD: 19.2% less electricity and 22.6% less water.
  • The models were also tested on 659 real buildings from open data.

Tool workflow

How we built it, and why these tools

Frames, then components, then the prototype, then handoff to code. The same order we would show live in the file.

1

Frames

One frame per screen: Desktop 1440 × 900 and Phone 390 × 844. Long screens scroll inside the frame; the sidebar and top bar are fixed children, so they stay put like in the app.

→
2

Components

Variables for 64 colours plus spacing and radius, nine text styles, and nine components (Button, Pill, Data source, Nav item, KPI card, Room tile, Alert card, Sidebar, Top bar). Each has variants for its states and is built in auto-layout, so it grows with its text.

→
3

Prototyping

Two flows: Estate officer on desktop, Caretaker on phone. Links use Navigate with Smart animate (200 ms, ease out). Every button, tab and slider dissolves into its after-click copy and keeps the scroll position. The phone menu is an overlay that slides in; the 3D view is an animated GIF.

→
4

Handoff

Colour, spacing and type live in Figma variables with developer-friendly names, and every hi-fi screen carries realistic data from the simulated block. A developer reads spacing, colour and type straight from the variables; nothing is re-drawn by hand.

  • Figma. Components, variants, auto-layout, variables and a clickable prototype in one file, shared with the jury by one link, free on the education plan.
  • Design tokens. One set of names for colour, spacing and type, so design and the future front end use the same values.
  • Python + SVG. DFDs, flows and sitemap are drawn from one script, so they stay consistent with each other and stay editable in Figma.
  • Simulated block. Every number on every screen comes from the simulated Academic Block A, so the prototype shows realistic data, not lorem ipsum.

Every screen explained (1 of 9)

Live building

The home screen. One look tells the estate officer what the block is doing now.

What it shows

  • Five readings for today: power now, energy and water used, money saved, CO₂ avoided.
  • A live 3D model of the block. Each room is coloured by what is happening in it.
  • Needs attention: the open alerts, each with its cost so far.

What you can do

  • Click any room in the 3D view to open that room.
  • Switch between 3D and a flat floor plan.
  • Fix an alert with one tap, or answer Right call? Yes / No.

How to read it

Green room: in use. Grey: empty. Yellow: wasting power. Amber sparks are electricity moving to each room; blue drops are water moving to the tank and floors.

Alerts

Every problem ECOGUARD has found, newest first.

What it shows

  • What the alert means, in one sentence.
  • Why ECOGUARD thinks so: the readings behind it, such as people seen and extra power drawn.
  • What to do, in English or Hindi, and the cost in rupees so far.

What you can do

  • Press the green fix button: switch a room off, reset an AC, stop the pump, send a technician.
  • Answer Yes or No. The answer is saved as a training label.
  • Filter by Open, History or kind of waste; download the labels as CSV.

How to read it

Coloured bar on the left: amber for power, blue for water, ember for the walls. A red High priority pill means a safety risk or fast loss.

Every screen explained (2 of 9)

Rooms

All rooms side by side, so the odd one out is easy to spot.

What it shows

  • People counted in each room.
  • Using: the power it draws now.
  • Should use: what the room model expects for those people and today's heat.

What you can do

  • Click a room to open its own page.

How to read it

A room is marked wasting when it is empty and uses more than 0.2 kW above its normal empty level.

Room detail

One room in depth. Reached from the 3D view, the rooms list or an alert.

What it shows

  • People, power now against what it should be, wiring resistance and earth leakage.
  • A chart of the last 24 hours, 72 hours or 7 days.
  • The open alerts for this room.

What you can do

  • Switch the room off (locked while people are inside).
  • Change the chart range.

How to read it

Orange line: power really drawn. Grey dashed line: what it should draw. Green area: people. Orange above grey while green is at zero is waste.

Every screen explained (3 of 9)

Cooling & AC

HVAC means heating, ventilation and air conditioning. Here it is the split ACs.

What it shows

  • Each AC room: temperature, humidity, AC power and people.
  • Rooms set too cold, or cooling with a door open.

What you can do

  • Reset an AC to 24 °C with one tap.

How to read it

One line per AC room. The green band (23 to 27 °C) is comfortable. Each degree below 24 °C costs about 6% more AC power (BEE).

Water tank & pump

Where water meets electricity: lifting water to the roof costs power.

What it shows

  • Roof tank and underground sump levels, and whether the pump is running.
  • Water used by each floor, hour by hour.
  • The lowest flow at night, which should be zero.

What you can do

  • Stop the pump or close a floor valve from an alert.

How to read it

Blue line: roof tank. Grey dashed: sump. Light blue bars: pump running. At night every floor's flow should fall to zero; one that does not has a leak or a running cistern.

Every screen explained (4 of 9)

Inside the walls

Waste no ordinary meter can see, because it happens inside the wall.

What it shows

  • Wiring resistance of each room, worked out from the voltage drop.
  • Earth leakage on each floor.
  • Leaks inside the wall: night water flow and rising leakage on the same floor.

What you can do

  • Send an electrician or plumber from the alert.

How to read it

Resistance rising above its installed value means a joint is loosening and turning power into heat. Leakage above 10 mA points to a damp wall.

People count

ECOGUARD never switches off a room with people in it. This page is how it knows.

What it shows

  • Live headcount of every room, against its seats.
  • A YOLO person detector that counts people in a photo or webcam frame.

What you can do

  • Pick a room and upload a photo, or use the webcam, to count it.

How to read it

Only the number is kept. The picture is deleted at once, in line with India's DPDP Act 2023.

Every screen explained (5 of 9)

Savings & carbon

What ECOGUARD has saved, in kWh, litres, rupees and CO₂.

What it shows

  • Waste caught and waste avoided, per day.
  • A full simulated year run twice: without ECOGUARD and with it.

What you can do

  • Read the totals; nothing to change here.

How to read it

Carbon = energy saved × 0.716 kg CO₂ per kWh (Central Electricity Authority grid factor). Money uses ₹7 per kWh and ₹50 per 1,000 litres.

Next 24 hours

How much power the block will use in each of the next 24 hours.

What it shows

  • Actual use for the last 24 hours and the forecast for the next 24.
  • The expected bill, the peak hour, and yesterday's forecast error.

What you can do

  • Plan ahead: cool rooms before the peak, run the pump when demand is low.

How to read it

Dark bars: what really happened. Light bars: the forecast. The forecast adds up each room's model using the usual timetable and Jammu temperatures.

Every screen explained (6 of 9)

How it decides

Every trained model in one place, with honest scores on data it never saw.

What it shows

  • What happens to one reading, in six steps.
  • Five models: room model, waste detector, people counter, real-building baselines, day-ahead LSTM forecaster.
  • The detector's report card, fault by fault.

What you can do

  • Jump from each model to the screen where it is used.

How to read it

Caught: share of hidden faults found. Time to catch: half were found within this time. Error: typical hourly miss as a share of average use.

Tested on 659 buildings

The same ideas checked on real meters from the open BDG2 data set.

What it shows

  • Avoidable electricity, cooling used while closed, leak nights.
  • Model report card on 2017, a year the models never trained on.

What you can do

  • Filter by classrooms, labs or offices and open any building.

How to read it

Every number on this page is real meter data, marked with the blue Real meters tag.

Glossary

Words used in this document

kW and kWhkW is power at one moment. kWh is energy over time: 1 kW for 1 hour is 1 kWh, about ₹7 here.
HVACHeating, ventilation and air conditioning. In this block, the split ACs.
SumpThe underground tank that the municipal supply fills. The pump lifts water from it to the roof tank.
Earth leakageCurrent that escapes to earth instead of returning through the wire. Damp walls and worn insulation raise it.
Wiring resistanceHow hard the wire resists current. A loose joint raises it and turns power into heat inside the wall.
CO₂ factor0.716 kg of CO₂ per kWh, the Central Electricity Authority's average for India's grid.
BEE 24 °CThe Bureau of Energy Efficiency's recommended default AC temperature.
YOLOA computer-vision model that finds people in an image. ECOGUARD keeps only the count.
LSTMA neural network that reads a sequence (here, 7 days of hourly use) and predicts what comes next.
LightGBMA fast tree-based model. Used to learn the normal use of real buildings.
CV(RMSE)Typical hourly error as a share of average use. ASHRAE Guideline 14 accepts below 30%.
BDG2Building Data Genome 2: open, real hourly meter data from 1,636 buildings.
Simulated vs realAcademic Block A is simulated in code. The 659 buildings are real meters. Every screen is tagged with which one it shows.
