1. Design goals and rationale

Our Round 1 plan covered every kind of building and every kind of waste. The jury's main feedback was that it was too broad. For Round 2 we fixed one setting that everyone can picture, one academic block with five classrooms, a computer lab and two offices, and designed for every connected energy and water waste inside it.

DecisionWhy
Building map is the first thing on the home screenA facility manager thinks in rooms and floors, not in meter IDs. Colour shows what is happening in each room (in use, empty, wasting, wiring risk), so the problem is found in one look.
Every alert says what, where, since when, what it costs in rupees, and what to doStudies of feedback systems show that people act on money and clear next steps, not on kWh charts. The rupee figure is computed in code, never guessed by a language model.
Advice in English and Hindi, one tap to switchFacility and maintenance staff on Indian campuses are often more comfortable in Hindi.
Two buttons on every alert: "Correct" and "Not waste"Each answer becomes a label that retrains the model for this building. "Not waste" also stops any automatic action, so people stay in control.
Automatic mode with a 15-minute grace periodFast enough to stop most waste, slow enough for a manager to stop a wrong action. It never switches off a room with people in it.
A separate "Heat in Walls" screenThis is our novel module. Waste that no normal meter shows (hot wiring joints, current leaking into damp walls) needs its own explanation, with a picture of how it is measured.
Energy and water on the same screens, with the pump in the middleThe pump is where energy and water meet. An overflowing tank wastes both, so we show them together instead of in two separate apps.
Savings page with a full-year before vs afterAnswers the estate officer's real question: did it work, and how much did it save?

2. Personas

Proto-personas built from our literature review of campus energy and water studies and the roles that run our own university blocks.

Persona

Facility Manager

Runs the block day to day; looks at the phone between rounds.

Goals
  • Know which room is wasting right now, without walking every floor
  • Act in one tap, and not be blamed for switching off a class
Pain points
  • Finds out about leaks from the electricity/water bill a month later
  • Guard rounds catch lights at 10 pm, too late
Served by: Overview, Alerts, one-tap Switch off, Hindi advice
Persona

Estate Officer / Admin

Owns the budget and the green-campus report.

Goals
  • Show money and CO₂ saved with numbers that hold up
  • Decide where to spend on repairs
Pain points
  • No sub-metering: one bill for the whole block
  • Cannot prove that an intervention worked
Served by: Savings & Carbon (before vs after), Forecast
Persona

Electrician / Plumber

Gets called after something breaks.

Goals
  • Get told before it breaks, with the exact room or floor
  • Fewer emergency calls
Pain points
  • Loose joints are found only after a burn smell or a trip
  • Leaks inside walls are found only when paint peels
Served by: Heat in Walls, work-order tickets
Persona

Faculty / Students (occupants)

Use the rooms; not dashboard users.

Goals
  • Room is ready and comfortable when class starts
  • Never lose power mid-class
Pain points
  • AC set to 16 °C, doors open
  • Lights left on by the previous class
Served by: Nothing to learn: ECOGUARD never switches off an occupied room

Page background, tiles

Each resource has one colour everywhere: energy amber, water blue, hidden waste violet. Text contrast is at least 4.5:1 on white.

Typography

Inter 20 semibold: page title

Inter 16 semibold: card title

Inter 14 regular: body and advice text

Inter 12 medium: labels

Spacing and shape

  • 4 px grid; card padding 16-20 px; gap 12 px; page padding 24 px
  • Card radius 14 px, button radius 8 px, pills fully round
  • One shadow level (cards only)

Icons

Lucide line icons at 16-18 px: Zap (energy), Droplets (water), Flame (wall heat), Bell (alerts), PowerOff (switch off), Wrench (ticket), Leaf (carbon).

Components

  • KPI card: label, big number + unit, one-line context
  • Alert card: kind icon, title, kind pill, status pill, place and time, advice, cost line, actions
  • Room tile: 4 states (in use, empty, wasting, wiring risk)
  • Buttons: primary, ghost, danger; disabled when the action makes no sense (e.g. switching off an occupied room)
  • Toggle (role=switch), tabs, select, meter bar

10. States and accessibility

StateWhat the user sees
LoadingSpinner with "Loading the building…" (announced to screen readers)
Empty"Nothing is being wasted right now", "No readings yet: start the gateway", "Before-vs-after not run yet"
ErrorRed banner "Could not reach the ECOGUARD server" with a Retry button; header pill "Backend offline"; live view falls back to polling every 5 s
Power cutHeader pill turns red "Power cut"; energy rules pause (meters read zero), water rules continue
  • Colour is never the only signal: every room tile and alert also has a text label.
  • All charts have text KPIs next to them; meters use role="meter" with values.
  • Keyboard: every control is a real button or link with a visible focus ring.
  • Readable sizes: body 14 px, minimum 11 px for chart ticks; layout works down to 390 px phones.

11. Tool workflow

We built the clickable prototype directly in code (React + Tailwind + Recharts), connected to live simulated data, so every screen shows realistic numbers and every button really works. The design tokens live in tailwind.config.js and are the same names as in this guide. To present the design file, the screens are rebuilt in Figma as follows:

  1. Make one page per screen in the sitemap, each a 1440 × 900 desktop frame, plus one 390 × 844 phone frame for Overview.
  2. Create colour styles from the table above (brand, energy, water, hidden, danger, ink) and text styles: H1 20/28 semibold, H2 16 semibold, body 14, small 12, label 12 uppercase.
  3. Build components with auto-layout: Sidebar item (variants: default / active / with badge), KPI card, Alert card (variants: energy / water / hidden × open / actioned / dismissed), Room tile (in use / empty / wasting / wiring risk), Button (primary / ghost / danger), Pill.
  4. Use a 4 px spacing grid: 16 px card padding, 12 px gaps between cards, 24 px page padding.
  5. Assemble screens from components only (no loose rectangles), so a jury member can click any element and see it is an instance.
  6. Prototype links: sidebar items → screens; room tile → Room detail; alert 'Switch room off' → overlay toast 'Switch-off sent'; 'Not waste' → card variant Dismissed. Use Smart Animate, 200 ms ease-out.
  7. Design the three states on Overview and Alerts: Loading (skeleton), Empty ('Nothing is being wasted right now'), Error ('Could not reach the ECOGUARD server' + Retry).
  8. Hand-off: turn on Dev Mode; tokens map 1:1 to tailwind.config.js names in the code.
